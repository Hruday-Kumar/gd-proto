# ADR-0003: Student accounts / authentication ("Auth")

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Category:** Auth (see `METHOD.md` for rubric)

**Budget re-check (2026-07-25):** re-verified against the tightened
zero-out-of-pocket bar (see ADR-0004/`METHOD.md`) — **still holds.**
Supabase's free tier (Auth + database) is confirmed permanent, no credit
card required to sign up or to stay on it, and commercial use is explicitly
allowed on the free plan.

## Context
Accounts are a foundational, cross-cutting requirement (`PlaceMe_Product_
Context_v2.md`): they're what let the app tell participants apart inside a
live room (so a transcript line and its feedback go to the right student),
and what lets a student log back in later and see only their own history.
This isn't a "highest-risk, least-familiar" area the way WebRTC/STT are
(`TEAM.md` names those two specifically), but it's still a place where a
first-time team can do real damage — mishandled sessions, password storage,
or token handling are classic beginner security pitfalls, so "managed over
self-managed" still applies here, just with less urgency than the real-time
layers.

Pilot scale (~5–10 concurrent rooms at launch, ~20–30 at 3 months) implies
a user base of at most a few hundred to low thousands of registered
students for a single-campus pilot — comfortably inside every mainstream
provider's free tier, so free-tier size is not expected to be the
differentiator here; fit for a beginner team and lock-in are.

## Options considered

| Provider | Free tier @ our scale | Beginner/managed fit | Mainstream & maintained | Lock-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Supabase Auth** ✅ chosen | ✅ 50,000 MAU free, permanently [[1]](https://designrevision.com/blog/supabase-pricing) | ✅ Drop-in hosted auth, well-documented, large beginner-friendly community | ✅ Mainstream, actively developed | ✅ **Best in class** — fully open source, genuinely self-hostable if we ever need to leave [[2]](https://www.devpick.io/tools/supabase-auth) |
| Firebase Authentication | ✅ 50,000 MAU free (phone/SMS billed separately, we don't need it) [[3]](https://blog.logto.io/firebase-authentication-pricing) | ✅ Extremely well-documented, huge community, arguably the most battle-tested option here | ✅ Mainstream, Google-maintained | ❌ Proprietary/Google-only; migrating off Firebase Auth later is a known, nontrivial pain point (multiple 2026 "how to migrate off" guides exist) [[4]](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/05/migrating-off-firebase-auth-11-critical-checks-before-you-switch-in-2026/) |
| Clerk | ✅ 50,000 MRU free (raised from 10K in Feb 2026) [[5]](https://saasprices.net/blog/clerk-free-plan-changes) | ✅ Best-in-class beginner DX — pre-built drop-in sign-in/sign-up UI components, very popular in current React tutorials/examples | ✅ Mainstream, fast-growing | ❌ Proprietary, no self-host path |
| Auth0 (Okta) | ⚠️ Free tier reported inconsistently at 7,500–25,000 MAU depending on source/plan type — smallest and least clear of the group [[6]](https://www.saasworthy.com/blog/auth0-pricing-plans-guide) | ⚠️ More enterprise/config-heavy (RBAC, rules, tenants) — steeper ramp for a first-time team | ✅ Mainstream, long track record | ❌ Proprietary, no self-host |
| AWS Cognito | ✅ 10,000 MAU free (new accounts) [[7]](https://supertokens.com/blog/cognito-pricing-the-complete-guide) | ❌ Repeatedly flagged as confusing: complex configuration surface (user pools, app clients, identity pools, triggers), documentation complaints, and pricing that "has about five different definitions depending on when you clicked Create Pool" [[8]](https://www.thestack.technology/awss-new-cognito-pricing-complicated-potentially-costly/) — a bad fit for a team new to auth entirely | ⚠️ Maintained but DX-troubled | ❌ AWS-tied |
| Auth.js (formerly NextAuth) | Free, self-hosted (no MAU metering) | ⚠️ Well-known in the Node/React world, but... | ❌ **Now in maintenance mode only** — original maintainer left Jan 2025, security patches only, no new features; the project's own community now points new projects to Better Auth instead [[9]](https://github.com/nextauthjs/next-auth/discussions/13252) | ✅ Self-hosted, no vendor |
| Better Auth | Free, self-hosted (managed add-ons from $20/mo if wanted) [[10]](https://better-auth.com/pricing) | ⚠️ Well-designed and the community's current recommended successor to Auth.js, but it's a **library we run and own**, not a managed service — we'd be responsible for the user/session database, deployment, and security patching ourselves, with no vendor safety net | ⚠️ Mainstream *for a 2024-launched project*, but far less battle-tested than Firebase/Supabase/Clerk | ✅ No vendor, but shifts real operational risk onto a first-time team |

## Decision
**Supabase Auth**, as a managed, hosted service (not self-hosting it
ourselves at this stage).

In plain terms: Firebase, Supabase, and Clerk are all excellent, well-worn,
beginner-friendly choices that would work fine — the free tier is a
non-issue for all three at our scale. The tiebreaker is the fixed
"cloud-agnostic, no hard vendor lock-in" constraint: Supabase is the only
one of the three whose entire auth system is open source and can be
self-hosted later if we ever need to move off the managed service, without
rewriting how auth works — Firebase and Clerk have no such escape hatch.
Auth0 and Cognito are ruled out on beginner-fit grounds (heavier
configuration, documented DX complaints). Auth.js and Better Auth are ruled
out for now because they trade the vendor's security safety net for
operational ownership we don't want a first-time team carrying for a
security-sensitive system this early — that tradeoff make sense for a team
that already knows auth well, not for this one.

**Side benefit, not a deciding factor:** Supabase also gives us a Postgres
database bundled in. The DB/storage ADR (a future session) will still
compare database options on its own merits — but if Supabase Postgres wins
that comparison independently, using the same platform for both would mean
one less vendor account and one less integration to maintain. Noted here so
the next session has the context; not pre-deciding it.

## Consequences
- **Positive:** Free at pilot scale and well past it (50K MAU). Well
  documented, large community, good fit for an agent-assisted beginner
  team. Genuine self-host fallback keeps us cloud-agnostic per the fixed
  constraints. Sets up cleanly for the DB/storage ADR.
- **Risk to track:** Supabase's free tier pauses inactive projects after a
  week and caps you at 2 active free projects [[1]](https://designrevision.com/blog/supabase-pricing) — irrelevant once we
  have real students using it regularly, but worth knowing during
  early/quiet development.
- **Compliance note (not resolved here):** India DPDP requires explicit
  recorded consent and gives students rights over their data (fixed
  constraint, guardrail #4). Choosing a vendor doesn't satisfy DPDP by
  itself — during Phase 1 build we still need our own consent flow and to
  confirm Supabase's data processing terms are acceptable. Flagging as a
  build-phase to-do, not an engineering blocker.

## Revisit if
- Supabase pricing/free-tier terms change materially, or
- The DB/storage ADR picks a database Supabase doesn't offer well, making a
  split-vendor setup necessary anyway (auth choice would still likely
  stand on its own merits), or
- We hit a feature Supabase Auth doesn't support (e.g. a specific SSO/
  enterprise need) — unlikely at student-app scale.
